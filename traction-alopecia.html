Traction alopecia: when braids, weaves and ponytails take your hairline

How tension damages follicles, how to spot it early, and what can still grow back.

In this articleWhat is traction alopecia?Early warning signsWhy does it happen?How to protect your hairlineCan hair grow back?
WDr. Wanjiru MD
Plastic & reconstructive surgeon, Upper Hill, Nairobi · Updated 6 Nov 2026

What is traction alopecia?

Traction alopecia is hair loss caused by long-term pulling on the hair roots. It is common along the hairline and temples in people who wear tight braids, weaves, extensions, wigs, ponytails or buns, and it is one of the most preventable causes of hair loss.

Early warning signs

  • Tenderness, soreness or headaches after styling
  • Small bumps or pimples along the hairline
  • Broken, thin hair at the edges
  • A thin line of hairs left along the very front of the hairline
  • A hairline that has slowly moved back

Why does it happen?

Constant tension inflames and weakens the follicle. At first the damage is reversible. If tension continues for years, follicles can scar and stop producing hair for good.

How to protect your hairline

  • If it hurts, take it down. Pain means too tight
  • Ask for lighter, smaller extension weights and looser installs
  • Keep installs to about 6 to 8 weeks, and give your scalp breaks
  • Avoid glue on the hairline and tight edges
  • Vary parting and ponytail positions
  • Moisturise and gently clean your scalp

Can hair grow back?

If caught early and the tension stops, often yes, over several months. Treatment may include calming inflammation, minoxidil and PRP. When follicles are scarred, a carefully planned transplant can restore the hairline.

Not sure whether it is traction alopecia or something else such as CCCA? The treatments differ, so a scalp examination is worth it. Visit the hair loss clinic page.

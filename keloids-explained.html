Keloids explained: why cutting alone often fails

Keloid versus hypertrophic scars, who is at risk, and the combination approach that works better.

What is a keloid?

A keloid is a scar that keeps growing beyond the edges of the original wound. It is raised, firm and rubbery, and may be itchy or tender. It is benign, but can be distressing.

Keloid or hypertrophic scar?

A hypertrophic scar is also raised, but stays within the wound and often settles over a year or two. A keloid spreads beyond the original wound and rarely settles on its own. The difference changes the treatment.

Who is at risk?

Keloids are more common in people of African, Asian and Hispanic descent, often run in families, and favour the earlobes, chest, shoulders and jawline. Ear piercings, acne, surgery and minor skin injuries can trigger them.

Why cutting alone often fails

Surgery is a new injury. In keloid-prone skin it can trigger an even larger scar, so removal without a plan has a high rate of recurrence. Better results come from combination treatment: injections, silicone, pressure and, where appropriate, carefully timed surgery with adjuvant therapy.

Prevention

  • Tell clinicians, piercers and tattoo artists that you are keloid-prone
  • Treat acne early, especially on the chest and back
  • Use silicone and sun protection on any new scar
  • Seek early treatment if a scar starts to thicken
